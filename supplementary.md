Supplement to *Symbolic Gradient-Weighted Regression: A Scalable, Derivative-Consistent Surrogate for CALPHAD Thermodynamics in Phase-Field Simulations* (IEEE BigData 2026, 6-page limit). It holds what the paper does not show: the full tables, footnotes removed from the paper's tables, and the original wording of text that was condensed. \section{Related Work}

```latex
\textbf{CALPHAD surrogates.} Laiu et al.~\cite{laiu2022neural} train
neural networks on CALPHAD-derived Gibbs free energy for ternary
solid-solution systems, an opaque rather than closed-form model for the
same task.

\textbf{Solver-loop-level surrogates.} Montes de Oca Zapiain et
al.~\cite{montesdeocazapiain2021accelerating} replace a PF solver's
time evolution with an LSTM, validated on the full PF solve; we validate
individual Gibbs-energy queries and a 0-D implicit solve.

\textbf{Gradient-enhanced local regression.} A first-order Taylor
correction around a reference point is closely related to
Hermite/gradient-augmented Shepard methods and Gradient-Enhanced Kriging
(GEK)~\cite{forrester2008engineering}, and closest in design to the
gradient-enhanced partition-of-unity surrogate of Bedonian et
al.~\cite{bedonian2023gradient}.

\textbf{Continuity of local approximations.} Shepard's inverse-distance
interpolant~\cite{shepard1968twodimensional} is continuous but has flat
spots at the data points; local or modified variants restrict weights to a
neighbourhood \TODO{cite}. Moving least
squares~\cite{lancaster1981moving} with smooth, compactly supported weights
is as smooth as the weight function. Selecting one nearest reference, as
SymGWR does, is a Voronoi partition with discontinuous weights, so this
smoothness does not carry over; blending several references (our
SymGWR-R-IDW and -CS) is the standard remedy, and we test whether it pays
(Sections~\ref{sec:continuity}, \ref{sec:newton}).

\textbf{Automatic differentiation.} An exact gradient can also be obtained
by automatic differentiation (AD) of an arbitrary differentiable model.
Giudicelli et al.~\cite{GIUDICELLI2024101690} do this within MOOSE to
compute exact Newton--Raphson Jacobians for phase-field simulations whose
free energies come from a neural network.
```

### S3.2 Methods
```latex
\section{Methods: A Global Polynomial and Its Local Corrections}
\label{sec:method}

All variants share one global polynomial $P$ and differ in how they use
the stored reference set $\{(\mathbf{r}_i, G_i)\}$, the rows used to fit
$P$. Features are scaled with statistics from the training rows only.
Nearest references are found by exact search in scaled feature space.

\subsection{Global polynomial}
$P(\mathbf{x})$ is a degree-5 polynomial in the eight scaled features,
nominally 1{,}287 monomials including the bias, fit by least squares
(minimum-norm solution, because the design is rank deficient;
Section~\ref{sec:identifiability}). Its gradient $\nabla P$ and Hessian
$\nabla^2 P$ are available in closed form. Degree 5 was fixed from
earlier validation-split sweeps (before the protocol corrections in
Section~\ref{sec:protocol}) and was not re-selected here.

\subsection{SymGWR-T: first-order Taylor correction}
For a query $\mathbf{q}$ with nearest reference $\mathbf{r}_i$, the
original SymGWR prediction is
\begin{equation}
\hat{G}_T(\mathbf{q}) = G_i + \nabla P(\mathbf{r}_i)^{\!\top}(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{r}_i).
\label{eq:T}
\end{equation}
(Earlier versions averaged $k>1$ such terms; we use $k=1$.)

\subsection{SymGWR-R: residual-anchored correction}
With the reference residual $e_i = G_i - P(\mathbf{r}_i)$,
\begin{equation}
\hat{G}_R(\mathbf{q}) = P(\mathbf{q}) + e_i = P(\mathbf{q}) + G_i - P(\mathbf{r}_i).
\label{eq:R}
\end{equation}
This is the polynomial plus a piecewise-constant offset.

\subsection{Blended residuals}
SymGWR-R-IDW and SymGWR-R-CS replace $e_i$ by
$\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_k(\mathbf{q})} w_j(\mathbf{q})\,e_j$, with $k=4$
nearest references and weights summing to one: inverse-distance weights
(IDW), or compactly supported weights (CS) whose support radius is set by
the distance to the $(k{+}1)$-th reference.
\TODO{state exact IDW exponent and CS kernel from the code}

\subsection{Continuity and Hessian properties}
Write $G_i = P(\mathbf{r}_i)+e_i$ and $\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{r}_i$.
Then
\begin{equation}
\hat{G}_T(\mathbf{q})-\hat{G}_R(\mathbf{q}) = -\tfrac12\,\mathbf{d}^{\!\top}\nabla^2P(\mathbf{r}_i)\,\mathbf{d}+O(\lVert\mathbf{d}\rVert^3),
\label{eq:diff}
\end{equation}
so SymGWR-T is SymGWR-R with $P$ replaced by its own first-order Taylor
expansion: it discards the curvature of $P$. Inside a nearest-reference
cell, $\hat{G}_T$ is affine, so $\nabla\hat{G}_T=\nabla P(\mathbf{r}_i)$
and $\nabla^2\hat{G}_T=0$. At a cell boundary between references $i$ and
$j$ the value jumps by $\hat{G}_T^{(j)}-\hat{G}_T^{(i)}$ and the gradient by
$\nabla P(\mathbf{r}_j)-\nabla P(\mathbf{r}_i)$, both generally nonzero.
SymGWR-R has $\nabla\hat{G}_R=\nabla P(\mathbf{q})$ and
$\nabla^2\hat{G}_R=\nabla^2P(\mathbf{q})$ everywhere, so its gradient is
continuous and its Hessian nonzero; its \emph{value}, however, jumps by
$e_j-e_i$ at a reference switch. It is therefore not continuous. The
blends vary their weights continuously, which removes most value jumps at
nearest-reference switches, at the price of gradient terms
$\sum_j\nabla w_j\,e_j$ that can be large.
Since $\partial H/\partial T=-T\,\partial^2G/\partial T^2$ for the
enthalpy $H=G-T\,\partial G/\partial T$, a surrogate with
$\partial^2\hat{G}/\partial T^2=0$ has no heat-capacity term in a Newton
Jacobian (Section~\ref{sec:newton}).

\subsection{What the data identify}
\label{sec:identifiability}
The OFAT star fills only the six $(T,c_e)$ planes. The degree-5 design
matrix has rank 96 of its 1{,}287 columns on every split (93--96 on some
composition-group folds), so the polynomial
coefficients are determined only up to a null space of dimension at
least 1{,}191, and the minimum-norm solution sets that part to zero. The
polynomial is thus nominally 1{,}287-term and 8-dimensional, but the data
determine only 96 directions of it. Derivatives along $T$ and along each
element's own axis on the sweep lines lie (to numerical precision) outside
the null space and are identified; derivatives in $c_{\text{Ni}}$,
cross-element first derivatives and all mixed cross-element second
derivatives are not (null-space share 0.71--1.00;
Table~\ref{tab:derivfid}). Our validation therefore covers
on-line behaviour only, and this dataset cannot test off-line behaviour.
```

### S3.4 Results (all subsections, including the Newton, derivative, interpolation and latency paragraphs)
```latex
\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Protocol and Splits}
\label{sec:protocol}
All numbers use a corrected protocol. Duplicates are removed
(7{,}938 rows), the feature scaler is fit on training rows only, and the
primary metric is raw-unit RMSE. Two earlier problems are fixed and the
numbers they produced are retired: the earlier headline MAPE was computed
on z-scored targets, and the earlier random split had exact duplicates in
train+validation for 61.1 test rows on average (3.7\% of the test set;
3.0--5.0\% across seeds); the deduplicated split has none. We also corrected the PINN baseline: its physics
loss penalizes $\partial G/\partial(\text{feature }0)$, which in the data
was the Nb fraction, so temperature is now feature~0. Kernel regression,
LOESS and GP train on at most 2{,}500 rows (random subsample). The GP's
$\alpha$ is tuned on a 1{,}000-row subsample and the model is refit once on
the capped training rows plus validation; this GP protocol applies to every
GP number below (25\,min timeout; no method timed out or failed).

Three splits are used, and the effective sample size differs.
\textbf{Random:} ten random 60/20/20 resamples. Their test sets overlap, so
we report consistency across seeds, not significance. It is the best-case,
near-lookup regime: a test row typically has same-composition stored rows
at adjacent temperatures.
\textbf{Temperature band:} one fixed test set of 1{,}568 rows
(a contiguous temperature band excluded from training and validation).
Only the train/validation partition varies with the seed, so the ten seeds
are effectively \emph{one} sample, their spread is numerical noise, and we
give no $p$-values.
\textbf{Composition group:} whole compositions held out, 5 folds
$\times$ 2 shuffles, giving five distinct test sets each seen twice.
We treat the two blocked splits as the central evidence; they remove the
same-composition neighbours at adjacent temperatures (temperature band) or
the same-composition rows altogether (composition group).

\subsection{Accuracy}
\label{sec:accuracy}
\input{tables/tab_accuracy}

converges in 100\% of runs. SymGWR-R converges in 91.1\% (random) and
93.9\% (temperature band); the failures come from small value jumps at
reference switches (99.9\% of individual steps converge). SymGWR-T converges in
0\%: inside a cell $H=G-T\,G_T$ is constant in $T$, so its Jacobian lacks the
heat-capacity term. Secant also fails (0\%), and Brent only finds a sign
change, not $|R|<\text{tol}$, with a median largest temperature error of $710$\,K
(random). The blends converge in only $0.4$--$9.1\%$ of runs because their own
curvature drives $C_p\le0$ in 12--21 of 150 steps on average. Splines converge in
53--75\%, because of rounding noise in the table; this is consistent with, not
contradicting, their value accuracy in Section~\ref{sec:interp}, since the
Jacobian needs the second derivative. An earlier synthetic
gradient-descent diagnostic is superseded by this test, whose update is Newton's.
```

### S3.5 Conclusion and limitations
```latex

\section{Conclusion}

Relative to a global degree-5 polynomial, the local correction buys accuracy
where a same-line reference is close: on the random split SymGWR-T and
SymGWR-R are both well below the polynomial and the GP. Under a held-out
temperature band the original Taylor correction (SymGWR-T) is worse than the
plain polynomial and the GP, whereas the residual-anchored variant
(SymGWR-R) is the most accurate SymGWR variant and keeps the exact polynomial
gradient and Hessian, though its value still jumps at reference switches. On
held-out compositions no variant is robustly better than the polynomial.
Cubic interpolation, where it applies, beats all of them, and blending
references does not help. In a constructed 0-D Newton test only the
polynomial converges reliably, with SymGWR-R close behind; SymGWR-T cannot be
used in a Newton solver. The validated derivatives are $\partial G/\partial T$,
own-axis composition first derivatives and $\partial^2G/\partial T^2$; a full
Hessian is not claimed.

\section*{Limitations}
(i) No phase-field solver integration was done; the Newton test is a
constructed 0-D problem, and full MEUMAPPS integration is future work.
(ii) One alloy system on a one-factor-at-a-time star: the polynomial is
identified in only 96 of 1{,}287 directions, the data are interpolation-only
apart from the temperature-band and composition-group holdouts, and
off-line behaviour cannot be tested. (iii) The CALPHAD table is rounded
(1\,J/mol where $|G|\ge10^5$), which limits second-derivative references and
makes spline Jacobians noisy. (iv) Kernel regression, LOESS and the GP are
capped at 2{,}500 training rows, and the GP is tuned on a 1{,}000-row
subsample with one refit. (v) The speedup over \texttt{pycalphad} uses a
different (steel) database in a separate, unpinned run and indicates an
order of magnitude only. (vi) Scaling above 6{,}350 references uses tiled
copies with 1\% jitter and tests index behaviour, not accuracy; GP/GEK
scaling was not re-run. (vii) The temperature-band split is effectively one
sample, and the random-split seeds overlap, so neither supports significance
claims. MEUMAPPS' aggregate query volume was unavailable.
```
